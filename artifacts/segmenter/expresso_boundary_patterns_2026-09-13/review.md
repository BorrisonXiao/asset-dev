# Expresso boundary-pattern study: evidence review

The author's three questions now drive the analysis and HTML: same waveform across task policies; same speaker and text across expressive renditions; phonetic and acoustic explanations. Recognition performance remains in the separately linked earlier audit. No manuscript changes.

## Supported observations

1. **Directional selection of shared transitions.** On 420 identical recordings, the count last-step checkpoint retains 74.8% of available ASR cuts near consonant→vowel transitions but 2.1% near vowel→consonant transitions. Emotion3408 retains75.2%/36.5%; intent last-step85.7%/56.8%. Count's rising/falling envelope-slope quartile retention is64.7%/4.1%. These are conditional retention rates, not phone-boundary precision or recall. Sources: same_audio_summary.json, extended_summary.json, tables/parent_anchor_decisions.csv. The direction persists with+12.5ms timestamps and across emotion seeds.
2. **Mostly shared, not exactly nested.** Count and emotion share82.7% of count's matched ASR anchors, versus46.0% under independent same-size subsets.17.3% [15.5,19.1] are absent from emotion;17.1% when count must be the sparser policy on the particular utterance. Thus strict global salience ordering is insufficient for these checkpoints. Shared initialization, rate bands and checkpoint stages prevent assigning this effect solely to task reward. Sources: tables/nesting.csv and same_audio_summary.json.
3. **Stable phonetic-relative placement across expressive renditions.** Emotion default-vs-happy/sad/confused F1 is75.2/72.4/67.7% after local phone mapping, versus36.4/36.8/35.7% for within-phone random placement with the same occupancy. Main-policy cut coverage is94.4–95.0%; no both-empty comparisons occur. Phone mapping aligns phone edges by construction, so it is explicitly a correspondence analysis rather than a discovery metric. Sources: same_text_summary.json, tables/same_text_pairs.csv, validation.json.
4. **Temporal flexibility.** Corresponding phones lengthened by≥25% gain0.218 cuts on average, compared with0.000 for phones with similar duration.20.0% have more cuts lying>20ms inside the phone, versus2.5% with fewer. The latter measure can include stretched-edge reclassification as well as newly added cuts; the total-count result is separately reported. Duration-only resynthesis corroborates flexibility: ×1.25 duration yields mean token ratios1.170 emotion,1.115 ASR,1.179 intent and1.079 count. Fixed landmark count and a fixed clock rate are reference behaviors, not trained baselines. Sources: extended_summary.json, tables/phone_paired_changes.csv, probe_summary.json.
5. **Pitch tolerance of boundary locations.** Emotion F1 versus sham is94.1% for−3 semitones,92.0% for+3,91.6% for flat pitch; token counts are nearly unchanged. Sham-vs-original F1 is95.3%, making small pitch effects hard to isolate from resynthesis effects. Duration probes give77.6/83.1% F1 after undoing time scaling. This concerns cuts only, not pitch content of the pooled representation or downstream emotional perception. Sources: probe_summary.json, probes/acoustic_qc.json.

## Concrete cases and counterexamples

- ex03_sad_00134, “Who stars in the Nutty Professor?”: emotion cuts at0.90s near /R→Z/ in stars; count cuts at0.98s near /Z→IH/ into in. A short illustrative case near typical parent retention was chosen to show non-nesting, not as a random sample. Exact raw times and all four tracks appear in the report.
- ex01_*_00264, “For the fresh face contest”: vowel entries in for and the are consistently retained. The main emotion policy splits the vowel of face in default at1.64s but not happy, although vowel durations are200/190ms. This exception appears in two of three seeds; seed3409 also splits the happy vowel. Duration is therefore informative but not a sufficient deterministic explanation.
- Same group, whisper: the short word the has no emotion cut, and articulation differs enough that a word in laughing has a different phone sequence. The report does not force these unmatched phones into correspondence.
- ex01_default_00319: a420ms quiet gap after street,31.6dB below reference RMS, is shown with exact flanking cuts. Silence absorption is descriptive; no new silence-token mechanism or sentence segmentation is inferred.
- All420 recordings and all60 same-text groups remain selectable in the two interactive explorers, so these examples can be compared with counterexamples.

## Validation and weaknesses

- Original input hashes verified;420 natural predictions,480 controlled inputs,48 batch-sensitivity inputs have sorted unique internal cuts. Independent assignment agrees with greedy maximum matching; stored time maps and inverse maps are checked. All54 manuscript snapshot hashes remain unchanged.
-351/360 recomputed original policy tracks match the earlier cache exactly; nine tracks differ by1–4 cut locations. A batch-size1 repeat of all48 conditions from the six affected recordings matches276/288 tracks exactly. Substituting those repeats changes any full60-recording F1 mean by at most0.107 percentage points. Do not describe BF16 AR inference as universally bitwise batch-invariant.
- 60 speaker×text blocks are the bootstrap unit, conditional on just four speakers. Six renditions share each default and are not independent utterance-level draws.
- Words agree in360/360 pairs;2306/2418 words have identical stress-stripped phone sequences;268/360 whole-utterance phone sequences match. Phone mapping uses only eligible words and excludes gaps. Strict whole-sequence, word-warp,40ms and+12.5ms results are retained, not selectively substituted for primary results.
- Natural pitch estimates can show octave errors. Controlled-probe QC provides stronger pitch evidence, but PSOLA and pitch flattening still alter the signal beyond an ideal one-variable intervention. No claim of unchanged perceived emotion is made.
- Expresso short reads have no speaker changes, gold syllable boundaries or prosodic phrase annotations. The count policy's onset preference is not evidence that it detects speaker changes. The study cannot validate sentence/phrase segmentation.

## Paper recommendation

Use a compact final experimental subsection centered on transition selection, one same-text example and controlled temporal flexibility. Proposed interpretation: shared acoustic transition locations with task-dependent retention and variable internal allocation. Avoid a new unit name, direct emotion-token claim, or causal superiority of these boundaries. Keep the current ASR abstract until the author reviews this proposal. A matched-rate/matched-training-stage task comparison is the highest-value next experiment for separating reward and rate effects; gold prosodic annotation is required before phrase/syllable claims.
