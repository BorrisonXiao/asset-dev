Task conditioning · FiLM and what each phase tunes

Every task shares one boundary policy; FiLM (feature-wise linear modulation) is how that one policy is told which task it is segmenting for. It is a per-task affine map applied to the encoder features before they reach the policy backbone:

\[ \tilde{x}_t^{(\tau)} \;=\; \gamma^{(\tau)} \odot x_t \;+\; \beta^{(\tau)} \]

with \(x_t\) the 1024-d WavLM-Large frame at time \(t\), \(\tau\) the task id, and \(\gamma,\beta\) two nn.Embedding(5, 1024) tables initialized to ones and zeros, so the whole mechanism starts as an exact identity. Three properties matter more than the parameter count:

  • It is time-invariant. One scale and one shift per channel, broadcast across all frames. FiLM can change which feature dimensions the policy attends to for a task, but it cannot say “put a boundary here rather than there” directly — placement changes only through how the frozen backbone reads the reweighted channels.
  • It conditions the policy only, not the representation. FiLM is applied on the path into the boundary backbone (boundary_logits, sample_boundaries, score_boundaries). The pooler is called as pool(features, …) on the raw features, so the decoder never sees a modulated frame. FiLM's only route to the loss is the discrete choice of boundaries.
  • It is 10,240 parameters total — 5 tasks × 1024 channels × (scale + shift), i.e. 2,048 per task — against 66,785,920 of routed LoRA on the decoder side.

Two things about phase B are easy to misread. First, “FiLM-only” describes the policy, not the segmenter: the shared pooler (1,149,440 parameters) is trainable in every phase including the bridge, because its trainability is set by the train_shared_pooler flag rather than by the phase. So phase A freezes the boundary policy, not the whole segmenter, and in phase B FiLM is only 0.9% of the trainable segmenter parameters. The decoder's proj and routed LoRA train throughout at lr 2e-4 (the warmup and joint decoder LRs are equal in this run, so nothing changes at the boundary); the segmenter group runs at lr 5e-5.

Second, phase B asks GRPO to move the rate using 2,048 numbers per task whose only channel to the reward is a discrete sample. That is the mechanism behind the degeneracy reported in section 0: the rate is flat for all 6,000 FiLM steps and 29% of utterances have all K=4 rollouts score identically. Unfreezing the last block in phase C multiplies what the policy gradient can reach by about 78× (10,240 → 800,000 parameters), and that is precisely when the rate starts to fall and the degenerate share drops to a few percent.
